# Performance baseline — v0.2

> Captured via `pnpm -F @adjudicate/bench bench` on M1 completion.
> Methodology: vitest-bench (≥10 statistically-converged samples); single-process, single-thread, hot CPU.

## Hardware

- Darwin 23.4.0 (macOS)
- Node.js 22
- (Measurement-specific CPU/RAM details vary by run; numbers below are
  representative of a developer laptop in the M-series Mac class.)

## Methodology

- Each benchmark builds fixtures **once** outside the measurement loop.
- 1,000 iterations per microbenchmark (configurable per case).
- Results in `ops/sec` (hz) and **microseconds per op** at p75, p99, p99.5, p99.9.
- No I/O — `adjudicate()` and `adjudicateAndAudit()` use in-memory ledger + `noopAuditSink`.

## Kernel hot path (`adjudicate()` pure)

| Case | hz (ops/sec) | mean | p75 | p99 | p99.9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EXECUTE (valid refund) | 2,215,134 | 0.5µs | 0.5µs | 0.7µs | 1.6µs |
| REWRITE (refund clamp) | 243,620 | 4.1µs | 4.0µs | 6.5µs | 19.4µs |
| REFUSE (charge not found) | 3,180,817 | 0.3µs | 0.3µs | 0.5µs | 0.8µs |

**Key observation.** REWRITE is ~13× slower than EXECUTE because the REWRITE
guard rebuilds the IntentEnvelope (sha256 canonical-JSON). This is the
expected cost — the rewritten envelope must have a new `intentHash`. Future
optimization could cache the hash for unchanged sub-trees, but at <7µs p99
this is not currently a bottleneck.

## Trace variant (`adjudicateWithTrace()`)

| Case | hz (ops/sec) | mean | p75 | p99 | p99.9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EXECUTE with trace | 1,057,090 | 0.9µs | 0.9µs | 1.3µs | 1.7µs |

**Key observation.** Trace allocation overhead is ~2× the pure path. The
trace array allocates one entry per evaluated step plus the matched entry.

## Envelope construction (`buildEnvelope()`)

| Case | hz (ops/sec) | mean | p75 | p99 | p99.9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sha256 canonical-JSON | 236,332 | 4.2µs | 4.1µs | 6.7µs | 21.2µs |

**Key observation.** Hash computation dominates envelope construction.
Same canonical-JSON path is used by REWRITE's rebuild — that's why
REWRITE perf tracks `buildEnvelope` perf closely.

## Full audit-emitting path (`adjudicateAndAudit()`)

| Case | hz (ops/sec) | mean | p75 | p99 | p99.9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| REFUSE (no EXECUTE → no ledger claim) | 150,736 | 6.6µs | 6.5µs | 9.5µs | 37.5µs |

**Key observation.** The full path (ledger consult, metrics, learning,
AuditRecord build, sink emit) is still 6.6µs mean. EXECUTE paths pay an
additional SET-NX ledger claim (~3µs); production deployments with real
Redis will pay network RTT instead.

## SLO comparison

| SLO target | Measured p99 | Headroom |
|---|---|---|
| `adjudicate()` p99 ≤ 2ms | 0.7µs (EXECUTE), 6.5µs (REWRITE), 0.5µs (REFUSE) | **>200×** |
| `adjudicateAndAudit()` p99 ≤ 15ms (with in-memory ledger) | 9.5µs (REFUSE) | **>1000×** |

All v0.2 baselines have substantial headroom against the published SLOs.
Production-shaped numbers will include I/O (Redis ledger, Postgres
AuditSink, NATS audit stream) which dominate over the kernel cost.

## Regression detection

For M1, regression detection is **manual** — re-run `pnpm -F @adjudicate/bench bench`
after kernel/runtime changes and compare against this baseline. Threshold:
≥+10% increase in p99 mean blocks the change pending ADR justification.

M3 ships a CI-side regression gate (`@adjudicate/bench` runs on every PR
touching `packages/core/src/**` or `packages/runtime/src/**`).

## Notes

- Bench numbers are wall-clock microseconds, single-process. Production
  numbers will be higher due to:
  - Real Redis ledger (1-5ms p99 RTT)
  - Real Postgres AuditSink (5-50ms p99 RTT, mitigated by `bufferedSink`)
  - JSON serialization at adapter boundary (variable, depends on payload size)
  - V8 JIT warmup (steady-state numbers dominate; first few requests in a
    container instance pay JIT cost)

- The v0.2 baseline is **comparative**, not authoritative for production
  capacity planning. For capacity planning, run benchmarks in your
  deployment topology with your real payload distribution.
